How to use the booth atlas

  1. Click any booth or its shaded area. Its full profile opens on the right (below the map on a phone): voters, men and women, an age pyramid, its streets or mohallas, and past results.
  2. Change what the map shows with Colour by: the 2022 or 2017 leader, party shares, INC change since 2017, voters per booth, women per 1,000 men, young and older voters, or new voters. darker always means more. For leaders, pale areas were close contests.
  3. Search by booth number, village, or a street or mohalla name.
  4. Layers turns on gurdwaras and temples, health centres, schools, water and sewage works, government offices, and heritage sites. It also has the Google Earth download.
  5. Inside a booth: hover the age bars for exact numbers, check how its past results were matched, and tap the official roll pages to enlarge them. They include ECI's own hand-drawn map of the booth area.
  6. Solid dots are ECI's own GPS points. Hollow dots are approximate, mostly inside Sirhind town. Booth areas are estimates from the nearest polling station, not official boundaries.
  7. Correct the map (on the team's Claude link, for members with edit access): move a booth to its real building, pin each street or mohalla, and add places. Everyone sees the changes.
